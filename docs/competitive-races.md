# How competitive were WFP's races?

Generated by `analysis/competitiveness.py` from results run 20261116T175634Z (2026-11-16T17:58Z). Counts are still unofficial in many places; races under a point can still move.

## Method

- **Margin:** WFP's pick against the rival across the winning line (first loser if WFP's pick is winning, last winner if not), as a share of the two's combined votes. For a two-way race it's the usual margin.
- **Bands:** tossup under 5 points, lean 5–10, likely 10–20, safe 20+. **Competitive** = under 10.
- **Kinds:** *partisan* (different parties, almost always Democrat/WFP vs Republican); *nonpartisan/intra-party* (nonpartisan offices, and same-party races such as California's top-two); *measure* (WFP's Yes/No position).
- This measures how close the race **ended up**, not how it looked beforehand. A seat rated safe that turned close counts as competitive here, and a targeted race that turned into a blowout doesn't.

**Coverage:** 1,085 of 1,221 endorsements have results. Not included: 96 no source, 39 not on ballot, 1 unmatched.

## Before the election: last result in the seat (state legislatures in twelve states)

Forecasters don't rate state legislative seats, so these use each seat's previous general election on the same district lines, for the twelve states with the most WFP legislative races: New York, Connecticut, Pennsylvania, Oregon, New Mexico, Colorado, Georgia, Wisconsin, California, Ohio, Arizona and Massachusetts. Lower chambers use 2024. Senate seats use the last time they were on the ballot, 2024 or 2022, since most state senators serve four-year terms. The margin is the top Democrat's votes (all party lines) against the top Republican's, from WFP's side, in the same bands as above. Only races with a Democrat and a Republican on the 2026 ballot are rated. California's top-two system means some seats had no Republican (or no Democrat) in the last general. Sources: the states' official results for NY, CT, PA, CO and GA, Wikipedia's district tables for CA, OR, NM, WI, AZ, OH and MA. Wisconsin's and Ohio's Senates are left out: their 2026 seats were last elected in 2022, on maps replaced since. Arizona elects two House members per district; there each party's strength is the average of its top two candidates. Most Wisconsin endorsements have no 2026 results source yet, so few are rated. Built by `analysis/prior_results.py` into `data/ratings/state_leg_prior.csv`.

296 of the 390 legislative endorsements with results in these states are rated; the rest are 83 uncontested this year, 9 same-party races this year, 2 contested races with no comparable last result (Ohio Senate, Massachusetts Senate).

Last time's result says nothing about who was running: an open seat, a new challenger, a seat no one contested last time, or an incumbent who changed parties (Cyrus Javadi, OR-32) can all play differently.
